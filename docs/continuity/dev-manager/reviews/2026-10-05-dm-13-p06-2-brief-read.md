## 3. Findings

### Item 1 — D1, the stages: approved

B before A would have no app path to exercise, and one session would mix an offline and a live review on one head.

### Item 2 — D2 and D3, the adapter and the suite: approved with conditions

**Is a guarantee shown "for the app" only by the adapter's run?** Yes, and the brief says so. The reference design's passes say nothing about the adapter.

**Does the reference design earn the job's time as a second subject?** Partly. Its value is its negative controls: they are broken variants of the reference design, and they are what shows the suite can still tell a broken design from a sound one. The app's adapter cannot carry fault switches without putting test-only branches into application code, which it must not do. Its full stress run, by contrast, repeats what P06.DB's record already holds.

**Conditions:**

- **2.1 No fault switches in the app adapter.** The controls stay on the reference design. The adapter's run has every case, the stress run with its floors and per-order counts (carried item 2), and the oracle. The oracle-level controls that do not depend on the subject also run against the adapter's rows, among them CX1's misattributed-row control (carried item 4).
- **2.2 The job's budget is stated before Stage A's prompt.** The reference design keeps its forced cases and all ten controls. Its stress run may shrink to what its stress controls need, if both subjects together would not fit the job's timeout, which is 20 minutes today. Stage A's prompt names the iterations per subject and the timeout. A slow runner must not make the job flaky (OD-21).
- **2.3 The runtime stays sealed, shown by a test.** The adapter lives in `services/api` but runs only under the proof's settings. An API test must show that:
  - the runtime settings and URL configuration import none of the adapter's modules;
  - the database backend is still the dummy one;
  - the connection refusal is unchanged.

  "Only the proof's settings run it" should be a check, not a convention.

### Item 3 — D4, session expiry: confirmed

Expiry is checked at a time read after the locks (P06.DB 5.2). O5 is narrowed to match, its detection of `transaction_start_time` is kept, and the boundary is forced with a delay between the check and the commit. That is the right rule.

Expiry is a soft, time-based boundary. A send authorized while the session was valid has not breached anything if it commits a moment after expiry. Enforcing expiry at commit would need a deferred constraint trigger the suite has never tested, for no safety gain. I do not ask for it.

### Item 4 — D5, the send's extra checks: approved with conditions

Fail closed until P07 sets Nathan's policy: agreed, per F06.

**4.1 Each new check's writer joins the lock protocol.** The pause, the profile restriction and the consent withdrawal each become a revocation of contact. In P06.DB, sign-out had to join the lock protocol (DM-08 5.1) because a check whose writer takes no lock can be read before that writer commits and still let the send commit after it. The same holds here. Each of these writers either:

- takes the account row lock in the canonical order and bumps the version the send checks, with forced race cases for it; or
- is excluded by name from DB06's ordering claim, with the reason.

The brief should also name the consent purpose that a send checks. "A withdrawn consent" alone is ambiguous, because consent is per purpose.

### Item 5 — D6, revocation and history: approved with conditions

Removing both members on an unmatch or a block, deactivating on suspension or deletion, keeping history at the provider for safety, and never using a ban, hide or freeze as a revocation: all agreed. They follow what I2a and I2b showed.

**The open question: per-user token revocation.** I agree with the manager's line (not on an unmatch or a block, where removal suffices and a per-user revocation would cut the user's other matches) and sharpen it:

**5.1 Revoke per user on every session-epoch bump, and bound the token's lifetime.**

- **The trigger is the epoch, not the event.** Suspension and deletion bump the epoch today, and P11's "sign out everywhere" and password reset will too (DB09). The Stream token must die with them, and keying it to the epoch covers the later events without another design pass.
- **A single-device sign-out** revokes no epoch, so the device's Stream token lives until it expires. The brief should state the lifetime, and it should be short, for example an hour. The client discards its token on sign-out. The residual, reads of channels the user still belongs to until the token expires, is recorded as a limit beside DB09.
- **TD-expiry is closed by removal and deactivation,** which end the open WebSocket on unmatch, block, suspension and deletion. Only the single-device sign-out case is left, and it is bounded by the lifetime above. The brief should say this, since the architecture document's section 8 leaves TD-expiry to P06.2.

### Item 6 — D7, the new model and migration `0003`: approved

The model is in scope: the adapter cannot keep provider identities opaque without storage. An additive, unapplied migration follows P02's precedent for `0001` and `0002`, and applying it stays P11's.

The checks must hold in both places:

- `makemigrations --check` passes in the API's static check and in the proof;
- `0001` and `0002` are unchanged;
- the proof's run applies `0003` from zero.

The ID is generated with `secrets.token_hex` and is never logged or shown.

### Item 7 — D8, what the client reads: the recommendation stands; not for Nathan now

**The challenge.** The alternative, no client token with Glow serving history and realtime itself, would close several residual paths by construction rather than by rule:

- S15's member custom data;
- `ai_indicator.*` events and membership state;
- the existence oracles;
- the WebSocket that outlives its token;
- every Video and Feeds path a token reaches, including the activities query.

That is real value, and Nathan's principle ("never any indication that there is anything happening outside Glow") leans toward it.

**Why it still should not go to Nathan now:**

- **It would undo a decision he made.** It changes ADR 0003, which he accepted as S15's answer (OD-14, OD-16), and the provider's role. Stream would become a message store, which reopens A08's choice of provider, not just P06.2's design.
- **It brings infrastructure forward.** Glow would need its own realtime transport and hosting before P09 or P11, and PF01 §6 warns against bringing integration forward without a concrete reason.
- **The recorded residuals are closed today:**
  - the server creates no invite, call, feed or activity;
  - the display rule and its test cover what the client could see;
  - IDs are random;
  - removal and deactivation end reads and subscriptions.

  I2b's review confirmed both design decisions that rest on these.

Under OD-32 an item goes to Nathan only with what it gains. Now it would gain closures that the evidence says are already in place, at a large cost. So the manager's recommendation stands. Two conditions make it hold:

**7.1 Record the triggers that send D8 to Nathan,** in ADR 0003's "Revisit when" and in the brief. If any of these comes true, D8 goes to Nathan in OD-32's form before the next stage:

- the mobile client cannot be kept from holding or rendering SDK state beyond Glow's own view model (section 8's open question);
- a client-reachable path appears that the display rule or a design constraint does not close;
- unread or read state needs a grant beyond `read-channel` (see 9.3).

**7.2 Prove the client half somewhere.** As written, no stage exercises the real Stream client SDK: Stage B is the server adapter, and Stage C is mobile on fixtures. So the hinge of D8, whether the app can keep SDK state out of what it renders, would first be tested at P11, the most expensive place to find out it fails. Revision 2 does one of these:

- **(a) recommended:** Stage C brings the Stream client SDK into the mobile app behind a chat adapter whose only output is Glow's view model (message ID, text, timestamp, own or other, delivery state). An offline test feeds the adapter the SDK's own event objects: `member.updated` with custom data, `ai_indicator.*`, a channel update carrying a name or image, membership and `pinned` or `archived` changes. It shows that none of them reaches the view model or the render. The new mobile dependency then comes to me with Stage C's prompt.
- **(b)** The brief defers the client SDK to P11 and records that D8's first trigger is tested only there, as an accepted risk.

### Item 9 — What the brief misses: changes requested, narrowly

1. **9.1 The Stream server SDK in `services/api`.** The adapter needs a client for Stream's server API, and `services/api` has none today. Revision 2 says whether Stage B adds the `stream-chat` Python SDK to the API's hash-pinned lock, or calls the REST API through an existing HTTP client. A new dependency in `services/api` is a significant dependency under my charter, so I read it with Stage B's prompt.
2. **9.2 The Stream client SDK in `apps/mobile`:** item 7.2.
3. **9.3 Unread.** PF01 lists unread among P06.2's deliverables. `glow-match` has `read_events` on but grants members only `read-channel`. Revision 2 says whether unread uses Stream's read state, or a read cursor Glow keeps on the server.
   - Stream's read state needs the client to mark messages read, which may need a grant beyond `read-channel`.
   - Any grant change is a change to the reapplicable plan in section 5. It comes to me with Stage B's prompt, and it may trip D8's third trigger.
4. **9.4 Channel naming and custom data** (section 8's open question). The server sets no `name`, `image` or other custom field on a channel or member: only the random channel ID and the two members. Channel IDs and the Stream user IDs are generated and committed before the provider call, so an outbox retry reuses them and stays idempotent.
5. **9.5 Who updates which documents.** These documents fall in neither list of owned paths:
   - the data model (`docs/architecture/data-model.md`, for the new model and the outbox flow);
   - the production contracts (`docs/architecture/production-contracts.md`, F10, F11 and F13, if any contract shape changes);
   - `docs/architecture/interactions-fixtures.md` (P06 and P11 handoff);
   - the P11 plan's DB06 and DB09 entries.

   Revision 2 assigns each, either to the manager's paths or to the stage that changes it.

Two more points, for the record:

- **Runtime.** There is no runtime for the delivery worker before P11. Stage A runs it in-process under the proof's settings, and Stage B runs it from the conformance run. The brief should say that, so nobody reads "a delivery worker" as a deployed component.
- **Stage B's live scope.** Its owned paths include `proofs/stream-chat/**`. Its prompt must keep the harness's guard, deny-lists, budget and cleanup as they are, adding only the adapter's conformance rows, with the C5 nits applied first, as the brief says.
