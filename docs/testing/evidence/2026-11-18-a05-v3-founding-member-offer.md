## 1. Method

As A05-V2's record (its section 1) did it:

- **Fetching.** `curl` with a browser-style User-Agent fetched each source into its own new directory in the session's scratchpad, outside the repository; each fetched file was hashed with SHA-256; no fetched file is committed. Pages that serve only a JavaScript shell (four support.stripe.com pages and one Stripe pricing view) were rendered with the pre-installed Chromium and the rendered HTML saved the same way; the tables mark them "(rendered)". No rendering was used to get past a site that refused automated reads (ftc.gov, nysenate.gov, docs.legis.wisconsin.gov stay unread; section 15).
- **Reading.** A Python script run with `-I`, kept outside the fetch directories, stripped the HTML tags and flattened white space; `pdftotext` read the PDFs; Apple's documentation was read from its `.json` endpoints and Stripe's from its `.md` variants.
- **Quotation checks.** A second script searched for each row's quotations, split at their ellipses, as one continuous string in the stripped text, and reported **exact**, or **normalised** where it matched only after removing a bill's line numbers, page headers and line-break hyphens, or normalising quotation marks; each normalised match was then read in place.
- **California's bulk data** (`pubinfo_2025.zip`, 1,292,263,530 bytes, `Last-Modified: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 05:26:36 GMT`, ETag `"4d06606a-6605e05841b00"`: the same file A05-V2 read) was read by HTTP range requests: the zip's central directory, the law section table (`LAW_SECTION_TBL.dat`, the same SHA-256 as at A05-V2) and only the members needed (section 3).
- **Two certificate chains completed.** `www.ilga.gov` serves its certificate without the Sectigo intermediate: the intermediate named in the leaf's own Authority Information Access field (`http://crt.sectigo.com/SectigoPublicServerAuthenticationCAOVR40.crt`, SHA-256 `8eb2f17d668941c39a7fca0cee127ae0ebaf444610631cca3cd19eab46c5824a`, the same file as at A05-V2) was fetched and verified against the trusted bundle (`openssl verify`: `OK`) before the Illinois Act was fetched with it. `www.cga.ct.gov` likewise lacked its GoDaddy intermediate; the dating-law subagent fetched the one named in the leaf's AIA field (`certificates.godaddy.com/repository/gdig2.crt`) and verified it the same way (`OK`). Verification was never turned off.
- **Searches.** The WebSearch tool found URLs only; a secondary page led to a primary one. A fact resting on a secondary source is marked **UNCONFIRMED**.

### 9.2 Whether the automatic-renewal rows reach a single payment

Quoted as found; whether a single payment for a plan with no end date is any of these is not stated in the texts and is not resolved here.

- **California (UST-29):** *"“Automatic renewal” means a plan, arrangement, or provision of a contract that contains a free-to-pay conversion or in which a paid subscription or purchasing agreement is automatically renewed at the end of a definite term for a subsequent term."*; *"“Continuous service” means a plan, arrangement, or provision of a contract that contains a free-to-pay conversion or in which a paid subscription or purchasing agreement continues until the consumer cancels the service."* (B&P §17601(a)(1), (a)(5)). A "free-to-pay conversion" is one in which a customer *"will incur an obligation to pay for the product or service if they do not take affirmative action to cancel"* after a free period ((a)(6)).
- **Colorado (UST-30):** *"\"Automatic renewal contract\" means a plan or arrangement in which a paid subscription or purchasing agreement is automatically renewed at the end of a definite term for a subsequent term or on a continuous or recurring basis."* (§6-1-732(1)(a)).
- **Virginia (UST-31, UST-60, UST-61):** *"\"Automatic renewal\" means a plan or arrangement in which a paid subscription or purchasing agreement is automatically renewed at the end of a definite term for a subsequent term of more than one month."*; *"\"Continuous service\" means a plan or arrangement in which a subscription or purchasing agreement continues until the consumer cancels the service."* (§59.1-207.45).
- **ROSCA (USF-16):** it reaches a sale *"through a negative option feature (as defined in the Federal Trade Commission's Telemarketing Sales Rule in part 310 of title 16, Code of Federal Regulations)"* (15 U.S.C. 8403), and the Rule says *"Negative option feature means, in an offer or agreement to sell or provide any goods or services, a provision under which the customer's silence or failure to take an affirmative action to reject goods or services or to cancel the agreement is interpreted by the seller as acceptance of the offer."* (16 CFR 310.2(w), eCFR current text).
- **USF-17:** the FTC's 2024 amendments stay vacated; the advance notice of 13 March 2026 is the latest FTC negative-option document read (unchanged from A05-V2).
- **What these definitions turn on:** a renewal, a recurring charge, a free-to-pay conversion, or silence taken as acceptance; a member already in OD-69's free trial, whose trial converts to a paid plan unless cancelled, is within the "free-to-pay conversion" words of California's definition and the trial rules of UST-29, UST-30 and UST-60 for that trial, whatever the member then buys (item 7 of the facts table).
