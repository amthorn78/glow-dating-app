# The store, legal and safety requirements matrix (P09.3a)

**Research, not legal advice.** This matrix records what official store pages and legal texts said when they were read on **1 November 2026**. It was prepared by a research session, not by a lawyer. It does not tell Glow what the law requires of it in any particular case, and nothing in it is a legal opinion. Whether to take legal advice, and when, is Nathan's decision (DM-31, on N4; brief D6).

- **Research date:** 1 November 2026. Every row's source was read that day unless the row says otherwise. **Owner changes:** GP-36 and G-13 on 9 November 2026, to P09.2 for both parts, after DM-45 13.1 read Android's Restore Credentials pages (the [SV1 brief](sv1-identity.md), D13). **Rechecks:** AP-50, AP-51 and GP-51 on 8 November 2026, for P06.3's brief ([recheck record](../testing/evidence/2026-11-08-p06-3-matrix-recheck.md)); all three confirmed, with no requirement changed.
- **Starting commit:** `f40f1d7c2c224d450132855bf3930009554fe142` (manager branch `claude/magical-wozniak-yfmmx2`; `main` at `fcd6ebc`, PR38's merge). The current-state column reads the repository at that commit.
- **Brief:** [P09.3a](p09-3a-requirements-matrix.md), revision 1. **Session record:** [`docs/testing/evidence/2026-11-01-p09-3a-requirements-matrix.md`](../testing/evidence/2026-11-01-p09-3a-requirements-matrix.md): method, every source read and unread, limits.
- **Candidate jurisdictions:** the US (federal, and the states that bear on a dating app), the UK and the EU (OD-50, N4 (b)). Nathan names the launch countries from this matrix before P07's brief.
- **Maintained after this item** (PF01 §7, "prepare and then maintain"): see section 10.

## What this matrix does not establish

- **Applicability to Glow in law.** Each row states the condition its source gives (a definition, a threshold, a trigger). Whether Glow meets that condition depends on facts not yet settled: where the business is established, the launch countries, user numbers, revenue, whether paid features launch, and how Glow is built. The matrix states conditions; it does not decide them.
- **Completeness.** It covers the topics PF01 §7 and the brief name, and the binding requirements found while reading them. It is not an exhaustive survey of the 79 legal systems on the candidate list (the US federal government and 50 states, the UK, and the EU with its 27 Member States). Member States' own laws, and most US states' laws beyond the dating-specific, age-assurance, privacy, renewal and platform-safety statutes listed, were not surveyed (the session record, section 6).
- **Unread sources and enforcement practice.** Sites that refused every automated read (ofcom.org.uk, ico.org.uk, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, congress.gov, uscode.house.gov and eur-lex.europa.eu) are listed in the session record, section 5. Where the same text has another official channel it was read there (legislation.gov.uk, Ofcom's codes as laid before Parliament on gov.uk, California's official bulk data, govinfo.gov, the EU Publications Office). Ofcom's and the ICO's guidance, and the ICO's Age Appropriate Design Code, stayed unread; rows that depend on them say so.
- **Store approval.** Meeting every store row does not establish that Apple or Google will approve the app; review is the stores' own decision (PF01 §7: a successful preview or test is not store-approval proof).
- **Costs.** Prices and the costed list belong to M07 (OD-50).
- **Design.** It does not design any feature; the owning phase's brief does (brief D6).

## 1. How to read this matrix

**Each row** is one requirement from one source. Its columns are:

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| ID | Stable identifier. AP = Apple, GP = Google Play, USF = US federal, UST = US state, UK, EU. A later revision adds IDs and marks superseded rows; it does not reuse them |
| T | The PF01 §7 topic: T1 distinctive dating value and how stores treat dating and astrology-like apps; T2 user-generated content (moderation, reporting, blocking, contact); T3 adults-only (age rating, age assurance); T4 child safety; T5 privacy disclosures and data protection; T6 account deletion; T7 sign-in; T8 subscriptions and in-app purchase; T9 permissions and purpose strings; T10 review access; T11 another requirement a source makes binding on a dating app |
| Requirement, with its trigger | The requirement in a sentence, with a short quotation of the source's own words in italics where the words decide whether it applies |
| Source | The official page or legal text, its section, the URL read, and the date read (all 1 November 2026) |
| Binds Glow when | The store or jurisdiction, and the condition that makes it apply: a definition, a threshold, a date, an injunction |
| Asks of Glow | What it would ask, concretely: a feature, a disclosure, a process, a report, a registration or a named contact |
| Owner | The PF01 phase or Work Register item that would deliver it, or **No owner**. "If paid" marks rows that bind only if Nathan chooses a paid launch (A06). "—" marks information rows that ask nothing |
| State at `f40f1d7` | What the repository does today, with the file it was read in: **met**, **partly met** (at fixture scope), **planned** (with the item), **not planned**, or **not applicable now** (with the reason). A fixture behaviour is never counted as the production obligation met |

**UNCONFIRMED** marks a row, or the part of a row, that rests on a secondary source or on a page that could not be read. Section 6 of the [session record](../testing/evidence/2026-11-01-p09-3a-requirements-matrix.md) lists them.

### Glow's facts used to judge applicability

These come from the repository at `f40f1d7`. Facts the repository does not record are treated as unknown, and rows give their condition for each case.

- **Platforms:** iOS and Android apps only; no consumer web app; WordPress is the public landing, policy and support site and the staff interface, through scoped Django APIs only (PF01 §1; OD-19).
- **Users:** adults only. Today the minimum age of 18 is a synthetic fixture policy applied to a self-entered birth date; no launch jurisdiction or production age policy is chosen (`docs/architecture/onboarding-fixtures.md`, F02; `apps/mobile/src/onboarding/policy.ts`).
- **Profiles:** photos uploaded into quarantine and moderator review before any approved variant is delivered (fixture; `docs/architecture/private-media-fixtures.md`); private birth date, local time and place (`docs/architecture/data-model.md`); a free-text `location_label` that the public projection does not expose, and no device-location permission (`services/api/glow_persistence/models.py`; `apps/mobile/app.config.ts`); preferences that are synthetic placeholders (`demo_a`, `demo_b`): no gender or orientation taxonomy is chosen (`docs/architecture/profiles-preferences-fixtures.md`).
- **Matching and chat:** recommendations, broader discovery, like and pass, mutual match, unmatch and block (fixtures, P05); one-to-one text chat after a mutual match, through Stream behind Glow's server, with no provider credential in the app (OD-45). After an unmatch or block neither person can send or see the conversation, which is kept out of sight only for safety reports (OD-12; the P06.2 brief, D6).
- **Safety and privacy contracts:** block (F13), report (F14), staff cases and appeals (F15), support (F16), export (F17), in-app and public deletion requests (F18) and entitlements (F20, every purchase denied) are defined as contracts (`docs/architecture/production-contracts.md`); their workflows are P07 and P08, and none is built.
- **Not present:** no tracking, analytics, notification, location, purchase or sign-in SDK (`apps/mobile/package.json`); no native build, EAS project, signing identity, store account or over-the-air update (`apps/mobile/AGENTS.md`, "Release block"); no CSAM detection or reporting design (M05 E6).
- **Operator and size:** one person, Nathan, holds every human role (OD-23); no users and no revenue. Where the business is established is not recorded.

### Summary

- **320 requirement rows:** Apple 66, Google Play 65, US federal 22, US states 51, UK 50, EU 66. Of these, 85 have **no owner** (wholly or for a named part) and 81 do **not apply now** (most bind only with paid features, social login, device location, a size threshold, or an EU size class Glow is below).
- **Child safety (T4) is a requirement in every candidate jurisdiction and on Google Play.** The US requires a CyberTipline report to NCMEC on actual knowledge (USF-01) and one-year preservation (USF-04); the UK requires reports to the National Crime Agency, with registration before the first report, since 7 April 2026 if Glow has UK links (UK-15 to UK-19); the EU requires prompt reports of threats to life or safety to national police (EU-11). Google Play requires published child-safety standards, an in-app report mechanism and a named child-safety contact for every Dating-category app (GP-18 to GP-23). No source read requires Glow to scan for CSAM at its size; EU law limits scanning of private messages (EU-26, EU-30). No item owns detection, reporting routes, registrations or contacts yet (gap G-02).
- **In force now and unowned:** the US TAKE IT DOWN Act's 48-hour intimate-image removal process (since 19 May 2026; USF-08 to USF-10) and the UK's 48-hour intimate-image takedown (since 29 June 2026; UK-05) (gap G-03); state online-dating safety notices and fraud-ban notices (UST-01 to UST-21) (gap G-04); store age signals under Texas SB 2420 (enforceable since 4 June 2026) (gap G-01).
- **Dated next:** the Romance Scam Prevention Act's fraud-ban notices (federal, from 20 October 2027; USF-18); California's age-signal law (1 January 2027; UST-35); Google's trader-status declaration (by 1 February 2027; GP-59) and zero-tap sign-in restoration (April 2027; GP-36). Section 10 lists every date.
- **Sixteen gaps** (section 8), each with the smallest change that would give it an owner, for the manager to route. The A05 facts are in section 9.

- **UK:** the Online Safety Act applies on "links with the United Kingdom": significant UK users, the UK as a target market, or a material risk of significant harm (UK-02). A UK provider reports all CSEA content to the NCA; a non-UK provider reports UK-linked content (UK-15). UK GDPR applies under Art 3(2) to a non-UK controller offering services in the UK, with a UK representative (UK-26, UK-27).
- **EU:** the DSA applies once Glow "offers services in the Union", which recital 8 says includes availability in a national app store (EU-01); a provider without an EU establishment needs a legal representative (EU-06). The GDPR's Art 3(2) applies on offering services, with an Art 27 representative (EU-33, EU-34). A non-EU controller notifies a breach to every authority where affected users reside (EU-45). Directives (consumer law, ePrivacy, NIS2) reach Glow through each Member State's law (EU section introduction).

### Age assurance

- **Stores:** Apple: developers are responsible for their own age restrictions and must use the Declared Age Range API where the law requires (AP-14), in Texas since 4 June 2026 (AP-15). Google: dating apps must use Restrict Minor Access, which relies on Google Account age and leaves existing installs working (GP-11, GP-12); Google says it "doesn’t mandate" the Age Signals API and that each developer must work out how the state laws apply (GP-15, GP-16).
- **US:** no federal age-verification duty for dating apps was found; COPPA turns on actual knowledge (USF-11). In a February 2026 policy statement the FTC said it *"will not bring an enforcement action under the COPPA Rule against a Relevant Operator that collects, uses, or discloses personal information for the purpose of determining a user’s age"*, on stated conditions; the statement *"does not create any substantive rights or entitlements"* ([FTC policy statement](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/coppa-age-verification-policy-statement.pdf); [press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children); read 2026-11-01). The state app-store acts use the store's age data (UST-32 to UST-36); California's SB 976 asks that the operator "has reasonably determined" a user is not a minor before an addictive feed (UST-37); New York's SAFE rules set accuracy benchmarks (UST-38). Colorado's safety policy must state whether and when Glow verifies that members are at least 18 (UST-20).
- **UK:** self-declaration "is not to be regarded as age verification or age estimation" (UK-13), and only verification or estimation supports a conclusion that children cannot access the service (UK-12). If children can access it and the child user condition is met, the children's risk assessment and safety duties follow (UK-14).
- **EU:** while Glow is micro or small, DSA Art 28 and the Commission's guidelines do not apply (EU-23, EU-24). Once they apply, the guidelines treat age verification as appropriate for an 18+ service and self-declaration as not appropriate (EU-24). The EDPB says age assurance often needs a DPIA and redress (EU-25). AI facial age estimation is biometric categorisation (EU-62). A Commission Recommendation asks Member States to make an EU age-verification solution available by 31 December 2026 ([Recommendation (EU) 2026/1035](http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32026H1035), read 2026-11-01; EU report, E2).

### The preference taxonomy (gender, orientation)

- **Stores:** Apple's privacy label lists sexual orientation under Sensitive Info (AP-23); Google's Data safety form has a "Sexual orientation" type, and data purposefully derived from other data must be declared too (GP-25).
- **US:** sexual orientation is sensitive data under California (if "collected and analyzed"), Texas ("sexuality"), Virginia and Colorado; consent is required at the thresholds, and in Texas before any sale even for a small business (UST-42, UST-45, UST-47, UST-48). FTC guidance asks for affirmative consent before collecting sensitive data (USF-13; guidance, not binding).
- **UK:** sex life and sexual orientation are special category data: explicit consent or another Art 9 condition (UK-29); significant solely automated decisions on such data need Art 22B conditions (UK-33); special category data removes the records exemption (UK-37) and is a DPIA trigger (UK-39).
- **EU:** data that reveal orientation indirectly (own gender plus who one wants to see) are special category data (C-184/20; EU-36); explicit consent is the practical route; Art 22(4) limits automated decisions on them (EU-40); the DPIA and DPO tests turn on "large scale" (EU-46, EU-47); inferring orientation by biometric categorisation is prohibited (EU-62).

### Resurfacing and rematch

- **Stores:** no Apple or Google rule on resurfacing or rematch was found; blocking must exist (AP-05, GP-06).
- **US:** fraud-ban notices presuppose records of who received (and, in the state laws, responded to) messages from a banned member, kept after unmatch (USF-18; UST-08, UST-14, UST-15, UST-17, UST-18). Colorado's annual report asks how removed members are kept from re-registering (UST-20).
- **UK:** no provision read regulates rematching; blocking is one of the measure areas (UK-04); storage limitation governs how long pass and block history is kept (UK-50).
- **EU:** recommender transparency (DSA Art 27) applies only once Glow is not micro or small (EU-22); demoting a profile on illegal-content or terms grounds needs a statement of reasons now (EU-10); users may object to legitimate-interest profiling (EU-39).

### The history policy (chat after unmatch, block, pause or deletion)

- **Decided already:** after an unmatch or block neither person can send or see the conversation, kept out of sight only for safety reports (OD-12). The open part is reads while a member is paused, restricted, suspended or has withdrawn consent, and deletion (the P06.2 brief, 3.1).
- **US:** a CyberTipline report triggers one year's preservation (USF-04); a government request, 90 days renewable once (USF-15); the Stored Communications Act limits who may receive message contents (USF-14).
- **UK:** after an NCA report, two weeks of chat logs and connections before the incident are kept for a year (UK-18); chat is regulated content within the reporting and complaints duties (UK-01, UK-07, UK-08).
- **EU:** if the chat is an electronic communications service, confidentiality limits storage and analysis (EU-30); GDPR storage limitation and erasure apply, with a legal-claims exception (EU-53); exports must not harm other users' rights (EU-39).
- **Stores:** Apple's deletion covers user content shared with others (AP-33); Google's covers the data declared in Data safety, with disclosed retention for legitimate reasons (GP-33).

### Retention

- **Stores:** both require the privacy policy to state retention and deletion (AP-27, GP-27); Google's agreement says "only for as long as it is needed" (GP-32).
- **US:** one year after a CyberTipline report (USF-04); a written retention policy if any child's data is kept (USF-11); California's renewal-consent records three years (UST-29); New York's paid members' material deleted with a certificate at contract end (UST-25); Texas's age data deleted after verification (UST-32).
- **UK:** NCA report reference five years, content one year (UK-18); OSA records have no stated period (UK-10); retention must be disclosed (UK-30) and limited (UK-50).
- **EU:** periods or criteria disclosed and recorded (EU-38, EU-43); storage limitation (EU-53); a 12-month cap if chat scanning under Regulation 2026/1881 is used (EU-26).
